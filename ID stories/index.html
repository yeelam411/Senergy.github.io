Introduction

Intellectual Disability is a neurodevelopmental condition that begins during childhood. Children with intellectual disabilities may take longer to speak, walk, or take care of their personal needs, for example like dressing or eating. It is not a mental illness, but a lifelong condition that varies in severity from mild to profound, requiring varying levels of support for independent living.

Levels of Severity:

  • Mild (approx. 85%): May live independently with minimal support.
  • Moderate (approx. 10%): Require more daily support and supervision.
  • Severe/Profound (approx. 5-6%): Require significant, daily support and care.

How Parents Can Help:

  • Focus on Life Skills, while academics are important, teaching "adaptive skills" (like managing a small budget or following a schedule) builds long-term independence.
  • Be Patient and Repetitive, new skills may need to be practiced many times. Use the "Tell, Show, Do" method: explain the task, demonstrate it, and then help them do it.
  • Use Visual Aids, like charts, picture schedules, and color-coded systems often work better than long verbal instructions.
  • Seek Early Intervention, the earlier a child receives specialized support (like speech therapy or occupational therapy), the more progress they can make.

A Note to Students!!!

Everyone learns at their own pace, and your pace is exactly where you need to be. You have talents that grades cannot measure, perhaps you are a kind friend, a great helper, or a talented artist. Learning might feel like climbing a steeper hill for you than for others, but every step you take is a huge achievement. Be proud of how hard you work!

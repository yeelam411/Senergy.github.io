Introduction

Specific Learning Difficulties (SpLDs) are neurodevelopmental differences that affect specific learning processes such as reading, writing, spelling, or arithmetic, even when intelligence is in the typical range. SpLDs are not a reflection of ability. They are differences in how the brain processes information, which can make school tasks more demanding without reducing potential.

Common Types of SpLDs:

  • Dyslexia: The most common type, causing difficulties with reading, spelling, and writing.
  • Dyscalculia: Involves challenges with mathematical calculations and understanding numerical concepts.
  • Dysgraphia: Impacts written expression, resulting in poor handwriting and spelling.
  • Dyspraxia: Affects coordination and planning, making it difficult to perform motor tasks.
  • Auditory or visual processing differences: These involve challenges in interpreting sounds or visual information.

How Parents Can Help:

  • Focus on your child's strengths and celebrate their achievements.
  • Use assistive technology and tools to support learning.
  • Break large tasks into smaller, manageable steps.
  • Maintain open and honest communication about their experiences and feelings.

A Note to Students!!!

Your brain works differently and that is okay. Some of the world's greatest scientists, artists, and entrepreneurs (like Albert Einstein and Richard Branson) have SpLDs. A different way of thinking is often a superpower in disguise once you find the right tools.

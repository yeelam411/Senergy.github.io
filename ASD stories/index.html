Introduction

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a disorder caused by abnormal brain development. Even after growing up, the intellectual level of autistic individuals differs from that of their peers, primarily affecting their social, language communication, and behavioral patterns. It is called a "spectrum" because it affects every person differently. Some students may need significant support in their daily lives, while others may live entirely independently but struggle with social nuances or sensory overload.

Key Characteristics of Autism:

While every autistic person is unique, these are common areas where they may experience the world differently:

  • Social interaction and communication difficulties: Difficulty understanding others' emotions; inability to perceive others' thoughts, intentions, and feelings. Difficulty mastering emotion-related learning, such as lyrical writing.
  • Restricted, repetitive, and stubborn behaviors: Limited language expression, frequent use of repetitive or stereotyped phrases. Paranoid behavioral patterns, such as insisting on taking a specific route or seat when traveling, and refusing to change current habits.
  • Short attention span.
  • Sensory abnormalities: Hypersensitivity or hyposensitivity to sound, smell, light, or touch.
  • Specialized Interests: Developing a deep, intense focus on specific topics, e.g., space, trains, coding, or art.

How Parents Can Help:

  • Create Predictability: Use visual schedules or "First/Then" boards to show what is happening next. This lowers anxiety by removing the "unknown."
  • Honor the "Sensory Battery": Understand that social interaction and loud environments drain an autistic child's energy. Provide a "quiet zone" at home where they can recharge.
  • Be Literal and Clear: Avoid metaphors or idioms (like "pull yourself together") that can be confusing. Say exactly what you mean.
  • Support Self-Advocacy: Help your child understand their own needs. If a pair of noise-canceling headphones helps them concentrate, encourage them to use them.

A Note to Students!!!

Your brain is wired for a different perspective. Being autistic means you might see details that others miss or think about problems in a way no one else has considered. While the world can sometimes feel too loud or confusing, your way of seeing things is valuable. Your "different" way of thinking is exactly what will change the world.

Introduction

Speech and Language Impairment refers to challenges in communication that are not caused by hearing loss or low intelligence. It can affect how a child produces sounds (speech) or how they understand and put words together (language).

Communication struggles are not a reflection of intelligence. A student may have brilliant ideas but simply lack the language tools to express them or the processing time to understand others.

The Two Sides of Language:

Language is usually divided into two main areas. A student might struggle with one or both:

  • Receptive Language (Understanding): Difficulty making sense of what others say. It might feel like listening to a foreign language where you only recognize every third word.
  • Expressive Language (Speaking): Difficulty using words to share thoughts, name objects, or put sentences together in the right order.

Common Types of Impairment:

  • Phonological/Articulation Disorders: Difficulty making specific sounds, e.g., saying "wabbit" instead of "rabbit", making speech hard to understand.
  • Stuttering (Disfluency): Interruptions in the flow of speech, such as repeating sounds or getting stuck on words.
  • Developmental Language Disorder (DLD): A hidden but common condition where the brain has difficulty learning and using its native language.
  • Social Communication Disorder: Difficulty using language for social purposes, like taking turns in a conversation or staying on topic.

How Parents Can Help:

  • Model, do not correct: If your child says, "I goed to park," do not tell them they are wrong. Instead, model the correct version: "Yes, you went to the park! Was it fun?"
  • Use the 10-second rule: Give your child extra time to process what you have said and to formulate their answer. Silence is okay.
  • Visual cues: Use gestures, pictures, or objects to help explain what you mean. This provides a visual anchor for the words they hear.
  • Narrate your day: Talk about what you are doing while you do it, e.g., "I am stirring the soup with the big wooden spoon". This builds their word bank in a natural way.

A Note to Students!!!

Your words are just one way to share your world. While it might feel frustrating when your tongue gets tied or when people talking sounds like a jumble, your ideas are still important. Many great thinkers and creators struggled to speak clearly as children. Finding your voice takes time and practice, and it is okay to use tools like writing, drawing, or technology to help you get your message across.
